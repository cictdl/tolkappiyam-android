This application does not collect personal information. There is no account, no sign-in, no advertising and no analytics of any kind.

  • The entire Tolkappiyam corpus is bundled inside the app and readable with no network connection.
  • What you save — reading position, bookmarks, progress, scores — stays on your device.
  • The app requests one permission, INTERNET, and nothing else.

Who publishes this app

The Central Institute of Classical Tamil (CICT) is an autonomous institution under the Ministry of Education, Government of India, based in Chennai. CICT publishes தொல்காப்பிய நுண்ணறிவு as a free scholarly and educational edition of the Tolkappiyam.

Information we collect

None. The app has no registration, no login and no user profile. It does not ask for your name, email address, phone number or any other identifier, and it contains no advertising network, analytics library, crash-reporting service or tracking identifier. Its only third-party software components are Google's AndroidX interface libraries, which collect nothing.

The app does not request, and therefore cannot access:

  • Location
  • Camera
  • Microphone
  • Contacts
  • Photos or files
  • Call or SMS data
  • Calendar
  • Advertising ID

What the app stores on your device

So that you can pick up where you left off, the app saves a small amount of information in its own private storage area:

  • the passage you were last reading;
  • bookmarks and any preferences you set;
  • your progress through the guided learning path, self-tests and word games.

This information never identifies you, is never transmitted to CICT, and is readable only by this app under Android's application sandbox. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data from Android Settings, erases it permanently.

Read-aloud (ஒலி)

When you press the read-aloud button, the app passes the passage to the text-to-speech engine installed on your device. Some engines synthesise speech entirely on the device; others, including Google's, may send the text to their servers to generate audio. That processing is governed by the policy of whichever engine you have installed, not by this one. Nothing is read aloud, and no text is passed to any engine, unless you press the button.

Children

The app is intended for students and general readers and is suitable for all ages. Because it collects no personal information, shows no advertising, contains no in-app purchases and has no messaging, comment or user-content feature, it presents no route by which a child's data could be gathered or a child could be contacted.

Security and retention

CICT holds no user data from this application, so there is nothing on our side to lose, retain or disclose. The information saved on your device is protected by Android's application sandbox and by whatever screen lock and encryption you have enabled. We retain nothing, because we receive nothing.

Your choices

  • Erase everything the app has saved — Android Settings → Apps → தொல்காப்பிய நுண்ணறிவு → Storage → Clear data, or simply uninstall.
  • Stop the backup copy — turn off Google backup in Android Settings.
  • Prevent the font request — use the app with the network off, or deny it network access in Android Settings. All content remains available.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes what it collects or transmits, this policy will be updated and its effective date changed before that version is published. The current text is always available at the address linked from the app's Google Play listing.

One point to settle before you submit

The Google Fonts request sends the device IP address to Google. Google Play's Data Safety rules generally treat data that is only used transiently to serve a request, and never stored, as outside the "collection" declaration — which is why No is proposed above. It is nevertheless a judgement call, and it is the only thing standing between this app and an unambiguous answer.

The cleaner course is to remove the request entirely. Android already ships the Noto Tamil typefaces the app asks for, so dropping the Google Fonts link would change almost nothing visually, would make the app make no automatic network connection whatsoever, and would let both this policy and the Data Safety form say so without qualification.
